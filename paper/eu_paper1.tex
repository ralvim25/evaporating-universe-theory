\begin{abstract}
The $\Lambda$CDM model faces two persistent tensions:
the Hubble constant measured locally
($H_0 = 73.17 \pm 0.86$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$,
SH0ES) disagrees at $5.8\sigma$ with the CMB inference
($67.36 \pm 0.54$), and weak-lensing surveys measure
$S_8$ values ${\sim}\,2.6\sigma$ below the Planck
prediction. We present the Evaporating Universe (EU),
an interacting dark energy model in which a
Gribov--Zwanziger ghost condensate mediates a continuous
CDM$\,{\to}\,$vacuum energy transfer:
$Q = -\lambda\,\varepsilon(z)\,H\,\rhoc$. The
dissipation fraction ($\lambda = 2/3$), logistic exponent
($b = 19/36$), transition redshift
($z_{\rm trans} \approx 5.986$), bare amplitude
($\varepsilon_{\rm bare} \approx 0.0543$), and effective
coupling ($\varepsilon_{\rm IR} \approx 0.0426$) are derived
from Effective Field Theory in curved spacetime; no parameter
is fitted to data ($\Delta k = 0$). The model is
implemented in a modified CLASS Boltzmann solver and
validated through six Cobaya MCMC runs
(${\sim}\,380{,}000$ total samples; all six converge at
$R-1 < 0.01$)
against Planck CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, Pantheon+ SNe,
SH0ES, and DES-Y3 weak lensing. The CDM drain yields
$H_0^{\rm LKI} = 72.71 \pm 0.30$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$,
within $0.52\sigma$ of SH0ES. The $S_8$ tension is
traced to a systematic bias in the $\Lambda$CDM lensing
kernel; forward-modeling the EU kernel reduces the
DES-Y3 discrepancy from ${\sim}\,2.6\sigma$ to $1.03\sigma$.
Including DES-Y3 data directly in the MCMC confirms
compatibility at $0.58\sigma$. A $1024^3$-particle
Gadget-4 $N$-body simulation validates the drain
signature in $P(k)$, and model selection yields
$\Delta\text{AIC} = -6.88$. We present ten
zero-parameter predictions for Euclid DR1, LSST Y1, and
DESI DR3, one of which --- the CPL equation-of-state
mirage ($w_0 = -0.855$ with CMB prior) --- already matches
the DESI DR2 measurement ($w_0 = -0.838 \pm 0.055$) at
$0.3\sigma$.
The EU resolves both cosmological tensions
simultaneously, with zero additional free parameters.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Two persistent discrepancies challenge the standard
$\Lambda$CDM cosmology. The Hubble constant inferred from
the cosmic microwave background (CMB) under
$\Lambda$CDM~\cite{planck2018},
$H_0 = 67.36 \pm 0.54$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$,
disagrees at $5.8\sigma$ with the local distance-ladder
measurement of Breuval, Riess et
al.~\cite{breuval2024},
$H_0 = 73.17 \pm 0.86$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$.
Independently, the amplitude of matter fluctuations
$S_8 \equiv \sigma_8\sqrt{\Omega_m/0.3}$ measured by
weak-lensing surveys~\cite{des2022} falls below the
Planck $\Lambda$CDM prediction at $\sim\!2.6\sigma$.
These tensions persist across instruments, calibration
methods, and analysis pipelines, suggesting a systematic
origin rather than a statistical fluctuation.

Numerous extensions of $\Lambda$CDM have been proposed,
including early dark energy~\cite{poulin2019_ede},
interacting dark energy~\cite{divalentino2020},
sign-switching models~\cite{akarsu2023}, and the CPL
parametrization~\cite{chevallier2001,linder2003}
recently favored by DESI DR2~\cite{desi_dr2_2025}.
These approaches typically improve one tension at the
cost of the other, or introduce free parameters whose
values must be fitted to data. No published model
resolves both tensions simultaneously with zero
additional free parameters.

This paper presents the Evaporating Universe (EU), an IDE
model in which a Gribov--Zwanziger (GZ) ghost
condensate~\cite{gribov1978,vandersickel2012}
mediates a continuous transfer of cold dark matter
(CDM) energy into the vacuum:
$Q = -\lambda\,\varepsilon(z)\,H\,\rhoc$, where
$\lambda = 2/3$ is the transversality fraction on the
spatial hypersurface and $\varepsilon(z)$ is a logistic
activation function whose parameters are determined by
the heat-kernel threshold, the CKN holographic
bound~\cite{ckn1999}, and the TT projection of the
ghost--graviton vertex. All five model parameters are
derived from Effective Field Theory in curved spacetime;
none are fitted to observational data ($\Delta k = 0$).

The CDM drain modifies the Friedmann equation at the
percent level, producing two observable effects.
The \textit{global kinematic imprint} (GKI) raises the
background expansion rate to
$H_0^{\rm GKI} = 68.89$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$.
The \textit{local kinematic imprint} (LKI), arising from
the KBC void outflow~\cite{keenan2013,wu_huterer2017},
boosts the locally measured value to
$H_0^{\rm LKI} = 72.71$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$
--- within $0.52\sigma$ of SH0ES.
The growth tension is resolved by identifying a
systematic bias in the weak-lensing pipeline: the
$\Lambda$CDM lensing kernel applied to EU-generated
data underestimates $S_8$ by construction.
Forward-modeling the EU kernel reduces the DES-Y3
tension from ${\sim}\,2.6\sigma$ to $1.03\sigma$; including
DES-Y3 data directly in the MCMC confirms compatibility
at $0.58\sigma$.
Model selection --- comparing both models on identical
datasets with the KBC void correction applied to
both --- yields $\Delta\text{AIC} = -6.88$ favoring the EU.

\subsection{The Gribov--Zwanziger ghost condensate}
\label{sec:gz}

The Gribov--Zwanziger (GZ) framework addresses a fundamental obstacle
in the quantization of non-Abelian gauge theories: the existence of
gauge-equivalent configurations (Gribov copies) that survive the
Landau gauge condition $\partial_\mu A^a_\mu = 0$~\cite{gribov1978}.
The resolution restricts the functional integral to the first Gribov
region,
\begin{equation}
\Omega = \bigl\{A^a_\mu \;\big|\;
  \partial_\mu A^a_\mu = 0,\;\;
  \mathcal{M}^{ab} > 0\bigr\},
\label{eq:gribov_region}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{M}^{ab} = -\partial_\mu D^{ab}_\mu$ is the
Faddeev--Popov operator~\cite{vandersickel2012}. This restriction generates a
non-perturbative ghost condensate
$\langle \bar{c}^a c^a \rangle \neq 0$ --- a vacuum expectation
value absent in perturbation theory.

Through Ashtekar's reformulation of general relativity as an SU(2)
gauge theory on the spatial slice
$\Sigma$~\cite{ashtekar1986}, the GZ framework extends to the
gravitational sector~\cite{capri2016}. The ghost condensate on the
Friedmann--Lema\^itre--Robertson--Walker (FLRW) background acts as
the order parameter for a cosmological phase transition: below a
critical redshift $\ztrans \approx 6$, the condensate activates a
continuous transfer of energy from cold dark matter (CDM) to the
vacuum.

CDM participates in this transfer because it is a gauge singlet:
carrying no Standard Model charge, it interacts only gravitationally
and couples to the bulk vacuum through the ghost condensate. Baryonic
matter, bound to $\Sigma$ by its
$\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\rm QCD} \times \mathrm{U}(1)_{\rm EM}$ gauge
charges, is protected from evaporation by those charges.

\subsection{The unique CDM--vacuum interaction}
\label{sec:interaction}

The modified continuity equations for CDM, vacuum energy, and baryons
are
\begin{align}
\dot{\rhoc} + 3H\rhoc &= -Q, \label{eq:cont_cdm} \\
\dot{\rhoL} &= +Q, \label{eq:cont_de} \\
\dot{\rhob} + 3H\rhob &= 0, \label{eq:cont_b}
\end{align}
where $Q \geq 0$ is the interaction rate. Total energy conservation
is guaranteed by construction:
Eqs.~(\ref{eq:cont_cdm})--(\ref{eq:cont_b}) sum to the Bianchi
identity
$\dot{\rho}_{\rm tot} + 3H(\rho_{\rm tot} + p_{\rm tot}) = 0$.

The functional form of $Q$ is the unique leading-order interaction
consistent with FLRW symmetry and decay kinematics.

\textit{(i)~Dimension.}~From Eq.~(\ref{eq:cont_cdm}),
$[Q] = [\rho]\,[T^{-1}]$: an energy density per unit time.

\textit{(ii)~Initial-state population.}~The interaction describes
CDM$\,\to\,$vacuum transfer. By Fermi's golden rule, the rate is
proportional to the initial-state population: $Q \propto \rhoc$.
This excludes $Q \propto \rhoL$ and mixed forms.

\textit{(iii)~Geometric clock.}~The factor $[T^{-1}]$ must be a
homogeneous, isotropic, first-order geometric scalar. In FLRW
spacetime, the unique candidate is $H = \dot{a}/a$. Higher-order
terms ($\dot{H}/H$, $H^2/H_0$) are sub-leading in the derivative
expansion; particle-physics scales break geometric universality;
the cosmic time $1/t$ is not covariant under time reparametrization.

The unique leading-order interaction is therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{Q = \lambda\,\eps(z)\,H\,\rhoc\,,}
\label{eq:Q}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is the dissipation fraction and $\eps(z)$ is the
redshift-dependent coupling amplitude, both derived in
Sec.~\ref{sec:params}. The interacting dark energy (IDE)
literature~\cite{valiviita2008, gavela2009, divalentino2020}
postulates this form; Eq.~(\ref{eq:Q}) establishes it as the unique
solution of the derivative expansion.

The coupling amplitude $\eps(z)$ is the order parameter of the ghost
condensate. Below the condensation threshold $\ztrans$, it follows a
generalized logistic profile with a strict Heaviside cutoff
(Fig.~\ref{fig:anatomy}(a)):
\begin{equation}
\eps(z) = \begin{cases}
\dfrac{\epsIR}{\displaystyle
  1 + \left(\frac{1+z}{1+\ztrans}\right)^{\!1/b}}
  & z \leq \ztrans, \\[10pt]
0 & z > \ztrans.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:eps}
\end{equation}
The cutoff encodes the physics of the condensation: the ghost order
parameter is strictly zero above the threshold, so no CDM is drained
at $z > \ztrans$. The CMB, big bang nucleosynthesis, and all
pre-condensation physics are exactly $\Lambda$CDM. At late times
($z \to 0$), $\eps \to \epsIR$ (full coupling); at the transition
($z = \ztrans$), $\eps = \epsIR/2$ (onset).

\subsubsection{Dissipation fraction: $\lambda = 2/3$}
\label{sec:lambda}

The dissipation fraction quantifies the fraction of ghost-field
channels available for CDM--vacuum energy transfer. Its value follows
from the transversality structure of the ghost field on the spatial
slice $\Sigma$.

Under the ADM $3{+}1$ decomposition, the ghost field
$c^\mu = (c^0, c^i)$ separates into a constrained temporal component
$c^0$ and three spatial components $c^i$ on $\Sigma$. The Helmholtz
decomposition theorem yields
\begin{equation}
c^i = c^i_T + \partial^i \varphi, \qquad
\partial_i c^i_T = 0,
\label{eq:helmholtz}
\end{equation}
where $c^i_T$ carries two transverse degrees of freedom and $\varphi$
is a longitudinal scalar.

Two independent arguments force $\varphi = 0$. First, the Landau
gauge condition $\partial_\mu A^a_\mu = 0$ projects the
Faddeev--Popov operator onto the transverse functional space,
requiring $\partial_i c^i = 0$ and hence
$\nabla^2\varphi = 0$~\cite{gribov1978}. Second, a longitudinal
condensate
$\langle \bar{c}^a_\parallel c^b_\parallel \rangle \neq 0$ would
single out a preferred direction, spontaneously breaking the isotropy
of the FLRW background. Both routes eliminate the longitudinal mode:
only the two transverse polarizations propagate within $\Omega$.

The GZ effective action confirms this counting. At one-loop order in
$d$ dimensions~\cite{vandersickel2012},
\begin{align}
\Gamma^{(1)}_\gamma &\supset
  \underbrace{-d\,(N^2{-}1)\,\gamma^4}_{\text{tree: }
    d\text{ modes}}
  \notag \\
  &+\;\underbrace{\tfrac{N^2{-}1}{2}(d{-}1)\!\int\!
    \frac{d^dq}{(2\pi)^d}
    \ln\frac{q^4 + 2g^2 N\gamma^4}{q^2}}_{\text{loop: }
    (d{-}1)\text{ transverse modes}}\;,
\label{eq:gz_action}
\end{align}
where the tree-level prefactor $d$ sums over all Lorentz indices and
the one-loop prefactor $(d{-}1)$ reflects the Landau gauge projection.
The gap equation $\partial\Gamma/\partial\gamma^2 = 0$ produces the
ratio $(d{-}1)/d$ as an algebraic quotient of two fixed coefficients
in the action. On the spatial section of FLRW ($d \to n = 3$):
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\lambda = \frac{n-1}{n} = \frac{2}{3}\,.}
\label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}

The stability of this result under radiative corrections
follows from the Slavnov--Taylor
identity~\cite{taylor1971} for the ghost--gluon vertex in
the Landau gauge:
\begin{equation}
Z_c = Z_g^{-1}\,Z_A^{-1/2}\,,
\label{eq:ward}
\end{equation}
where $Z_c$, $Z_g$, and $Z_A$ are the renormalization
constants of the ghost field, coupling, and gluon field,
respectively. This identity constrains the ghost
self-energy to all orders: the transverse fraction
$(n{-}1)/n$ entering the gap equation is not an
artifact of the one-loop truncation but a consequence
of the gauge structure itself. Dudal
et~al.~\cite{dudal2008} verified that the refined
Gribov--Zwanziger action preserves this identity, so
the channel counting and the gap equation are anchored
to the same algebraic structure. Any higher-loop
correction that modified $\lambda$ would violate
Eq.~(\ref{eq:ward}).

\subsubsection{Logistic exponent: $b = 19/36$}
\label{sec:b}

The exponent $b$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:eps}) controls the sharpness of the
condensation transition. It equals the fraction of the ghost-graviton
interaction that couples to the physical (transverse-traceless)
graviton polarizations in $d = 4$ spacetime.

The ghost-graviton vertex
$V^\mu_{\;\nu;\rho\sigma}(p,q)$ from linearized quantum
gravity~\cite{codello2009} couples the Faddeev--Popov ghost to the
symmetric graviton tensor. The physical content is extracted by
projecting onto the transverse-traceless (TT) sector via the York
projector
$\Pi^{\rm TT}_{\rho\sigma,\lambda\tau}(k)$~\cite{york1973}. The TT
fraction is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{b = \frac{{\rm Tr}\!\left(
  V \cdot \Pi^{\rm TT} \cdot V\right)}{{\rm Tr}\!\left(
  V \cdot \mathbb{I} \cdot V\right)} = \frac{19}{36}\,,}
\label{eq:b}
\end{equation}
verified with irreducible exact rational arithmetic across four
independent momentum configurations (Appendix~\ref{app:tt_vertex}). The
numerator 19 is prime, confirming irreducibility --- a genuine
$d = 4$ tensor invariant.

The TT fraction governs the logistic activation through the
renormalization group (RG). Only the TT-projected component of the
vertex drives the condensate running --- analogous to active quark
flavors modifying the QCD $\beta$-function --- so the ghost
self-energy acquires the anomalous dimension $b$, yielding the Wilson
critical exponent $\nu = 1/b = 36/19$. The logistic $\beta$-function,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\sigma}{d\ln\mu} =
  -\frac{1}{b}\,\sigma\!\left(1 -
  \frac{\sigma}{\sigma_*}\right),
\label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
is exact at the upper critical dimension $d = 4$: higher-order
corrections are strictly irrelevant by the Ginzburg criterion,
protecting the universality of the logistic form. The RG scale maps
to redshift through the CKN saturation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:ztrans}):
$\Lambda_{\rm UV}^4 = \rho_{\rm crit} \propto H^2$ implies
$\mu \propto H^{1/2}$, a monotonically increasing function of
$(1{+}z)$. The logistic solution depends only on the ratio
$(1{+}z)/(1{+}\ztrans)$, rendering the result robust against the
precise functional form of $\mu(z)$.

\subsubsection{Condensation threshold: $\ztrans \approx 5.986$}
\label{sec:ztrans}

The transition redshift marks the onset of the ghost condensation. It
is determined by a closed chain of five links, each independently
established.

\textit{Link~1: One-loop amplitude.}~The universal one-loop prefactor
from the Seeley--DeWitt heat-kernel expansion~\cite{vassilevich2003}
in $d = 4$ dimensions is
\begin{equation}
(4\pi)^{-d/2}\big|_{d=4} = \frac{1}{16\pi^2} \approx 0.00633\,.
\label{eq:hk_prefactor}
\end{equation}
This is the standard phase-space suppression of a single quantum loop,
appearing identically in dimensional regularization, the
Coleman--Weinberg potential, and the ABJ anomaly.

\textit{Link~2: Holographic bound.}~Cohen, Kaplan, and
Nelson~\cite{ckn1999} established that an effective field theory
in a region of size $L$ must satisfy
$L^3\Lambda_{\rm UV}^4 \lesssim L \MPl^2$. Applied to the Hubble
sphere ($L = H^{-1}$) with the exact Schwarzschild condition:
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{\rm UV}^4 \leq
  \frac{3\MPl^2 H^2}{8\pi} = \rho_{\rm crit}\,.
\label{eq:ckn}
\end{equation}

\textit{Link~3: Saturation.}~The CKN bound
(Eq.~\ref{eq:ckn}) is formally an inequality. In a spatially
flat universe
($\Omega_k = 0.001 \pm 0.002$~\cite{planck2018}), the
late-time de~Sitter attractor drives
$\Omega_\Lambda \to 1$, saturating the Gibbons--Hawking
entropy bound $S = A/(4G)$ as a thermodynamic
identity~\cite{gibbons_hawking1977}. The CKN energy bound is
the effective field theory formulation of this holographic
constraint~\cite{ckn1999}; the inequality becomes an equality:
$\Lambda_{\rm UV}^4 = \rho_{\rm crit}$. Conversely, exact
saturation requires $\Omega_k = 0$: any spatial curvature
introduces a time-dependent correction
$\propto k/(a^2 H^2)$, breaking the constancy of the
bound. The observed flatness and CKN saturation are
mutually required.

\textit{Link~4: Threshold.}~With
$\Lambda_{\rm UV}^4 = \rho_{\rm crit}$ (Link~3), the
cosmological density parameter
$\Omega_{\rm DE} \equiv \rho_\Lambda/\rho_{\rm crit}$
measures the vacuum energy as a fraction of the total UV
phase space. Combining with Link~1, the condensate
nucleates when this fraction reaches the one-loop
amplitude:
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{\rm DE}(\ztrans) = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\,.
\label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}

\textit{Link~5: Solution.}~In the matter-dominated $\Lambda$CDM
background (valid at $z \sim 6$ where EU and radiation corrections
are negligible):
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ztrans = \left[
  \frac{\OL\,(16\pi^2 - 1)}{\Om}\right]^{1/3} - 1 \approx 5.986\,.}
\label{eq:ztrans}
\end{equation}
The inputs are $\OL = 0.6847$ and $\Om = 0.3153$ from
Planck~2018~\cite{planck2018}. Because $\eps(z) = 0$ for
$z > \ztrans$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:eps}), the pre-condensation background
is rigorously $\Lambda$CDM; Eq.~(\ref{eq:ztrans}) therefore evaluates
exactly using the primordial densities. The sensitivity to
$h = H_0/100$ is weak: $\ztrans$ varies by $\sim 6\%$ over
$h \in [0.66, 0.70]$
(Appendix~\ref{app:ztrans_sensitivity}).

\subsubsection{Coupling amplitude: $\epsbare$ and $\epsIR$}
\label{sec:eps}

The coupling amplitude has two components: a bare value set by the
vacuum channel counting and an infrared screening from the Gribov
horizon.

\textit{Bare coupling.}~The Gribov--Zwanziger framework
applies to any gauge theory formulated in terms of a
connection. General relativity on $\Sigma$, as
conventionally written in metric variables, is not a
gauge theory in this sense. Ashtekar's canonical
reformulation~\cite{ashtekar1986} resolves this by
recasting the gravitational phase space in terms of an
SU(2) connection $A_a^i$ and a densitized triad
$\tilde{E}^a_i$ on the spatial slice. Einstein's
constraints become polynomial in these variables, and
the internal gauge group is SU(2) with
$\dim(\mathrm{SU}(2)) = n = 3$. The existence of Gribov
copies is a topological property of all non-Abelian gauge
theories in the Landau gauge, independent of the specific
gauge group~\cite{vandersickel2012}. This SU(2)
identification therefore places the gravitational sector
on the same formal footing as QCD, making the
Faddeev--Popov operator, the Gribov region $\Omega$, and
its boundary $\partial\Omega$ well-defined.

Within this gauge-theoretic formulation, the GZ
localizing field
$\varphi_a^{ij}$~\cite{zwanziger1989} --- an auxiliary
tensor coupling the ghost ($c^i$) and anti-ghost
($\bar{c}^j$) to the gauge index $a$ --- possesses three
independent indices, each running from 1 to $n$. In the
deep UV, where the ghost and anti-ghost are distinct
fermionic fields with unrestricted quantum numbers
(Zwanziger's independent-index
theorem~\cite{zwanziger1989}), the auxiliary field
fluctuates as a fully general rank-3 tensor:
$N_{\rm bare} = n^3 = 27$ bare channels. In the
classical IR, the metric symmetry
$\gamma_{ij} = \gamma_{ji}$ constrains the ghost
indices, reducing the auxiliary field to
$n\,n(n{+}1)/2$ independent components:
$N_{\rm classical} = 18$ for $n = 3$.

The Gribov projection connects these two regimes.
Setting the effective count
$\lambda\, n^3 = n^2(n{-}1)$ equal to the
classical count:
\begin{equation}
n^2(n{-}1) = \frac{n^2(n{+}1)}{2}
\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
\boxed{n = 3\,.}
\label{eq:n3}
\end{equation}
The quantum vacuum recovers the classical limit only in
three spatial dimensions: $(2/3)(27) = 18
= 3(3{+}1)/2 \cdot 3$. This consistency
condition is not imposed; it is a consequence of
requiring the GZ ghost sector to reproduce the correct
number of gravitational degrees of freedom.

\textit{Democratic coupling.}~CDM carries no Standard
Model gauge charge and, crucially, no internal SU(2)
quantum numbers under the Ashtekar connection. It is a
gauge singlet. By the Wigner--Eckart theorem, the
matrix element of any gauge-invariant operator between
singlet states is proportional to the identity on the
mediator space: no channel is preferred over any other.
CDM therefore couples with equal amplitude to all 27
bare channels. The transverse one-loop factor
$(4\pi)^{-1}$ --- reflecting the restriction of the
ghost dynamics to the two-dimensional transverse plane
on $\Sigma$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:lambda}), in contrast to
the bulk factor $(4\pi)^{-2}$ used for the condensation
threshold --- distributes uniformly over the bare
modes, yielding the phase-space fraction
$1/(4\pi\,n^3)$. By Fermi's golden rule, the
transition rate scales as $|\varepsilon|^2$; the
coupling amplitude is therefore the square root of
this fraction:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\epsbare = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\, n^3}}
  = \frac{1}{\sqrt{108\pi}} \approx 0.05429\,.}
\label{eq:epsbare}
\end{equation}

\textit{IR screening.}~In the deep infrared, the Gribov horizon
modifies the ghost propagator from
$D_{\rm UV}(k) = 1/(k^2 + \gamma^2)$ to
$D_{\rm GZ}(k) = k^2/(k^4 + \gamma^4)$. This deformation of
the internal propagator acts as a vertex renormalization,
screening the bare coupling by a calculable factor. The
ratio of integrated propagator densities on the
two-dimensional transverse momentum
plane~\cite{zwanziger1991} evaluates exactly to
(Appendix~\ref{app:gribov_integral})
\begin{equation}
\frac{I_{\rm IR}}{I_{\rm UV}} = \frac{\pi}{4}\,.
\label{eq:screening}
\end{equation}
The effective coupling is therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\epsIR = \frac{\pi}{4}\,\epsbare \approx 0.04264\,.}
\label{eq:epsIR}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Modified Friedmann equation}
\label{sec:friedmann_eu}

The CDM density evolves under the combined effect of Hubble dilution
and vacuum transfer. Integrating Eq.~(\ref{eq:cont_cdm}):
\begin{equation}
\rhoc(a) = \rho_{c,0}\,a^{-3}\,\fsurv(a),
\label{eq:rho_c}
\end{equation}
where the survival fraction is
\begin{equation}
\fsurv(a) = \exp\!\left[
  -\lambda \int_0^{a}
  \eps(a')\,\frac{da'}{a'}\right].
\label{eq:fsurv}
\end{equation}

The Heaviside cutoff in Eq.~(\ref{eq:eps}) renders the total
accumulated drain analytically tractable. The kinematic integral is
\begin{equation}
\Ikin = \epsIR\,b\left[
  \ln\!\left(1 + (1{+}\ztrans)^{1/b}\right) - \ln 2\right]
  \approx 0.0679\,,
\label{eq:Ikin}
\end{equation}
where the $-\ln 2$ arises from the upper limit of integration at
$\ztrans$. The CDM survival fraction at $z = 0$ is
\begin{equation}
\fsurv(0) = e^{-\lambda\Ikin} \approx 0.9558\,,
\label{eq:fsurv0}
\end{equation}
corresponding to a $4.4\%$ transfer of CDM energy to the vacuum since
$z \approx 6$.

The modified Friedmann equation is
\begin{align}
H^2(a) = H_0^2\big[
  &\Omega_{\rm r}\,a^{-4}
  + \Omega_{\rm b}\,a^{-3}
  \notag \\
  &+ \Ocdm\,a^{-3}\,\fsurv(a)
  + \OL(a)\big],
\label{eq:friedmann}
\end{align}
where $\Omega_{\rm r}$ encodes photons and relativistic neutrinos
(one massive species with $\sum m_\nu = 0.06$~eV~\cite{planck2018}),
$\Ocdm$ denotes the pre-drain CDM density parameter anchored by the
CMB ($\eps = 0$ at $z > \ztrans$, so $\Ocdm$ retains its
$\Lambda$CDM value), and $\OL(a)$ is obtained by integrating
Eq.~(\ref{eq:cont_de}) numerically. The CDM and vacuum terms do not cancel: drained CDM
energy acquires the vacuum equation of state ($w = -1$) and ceases to
dilute, generating a net modification to $H(z)$. The full numerical
integration is performed by the CLASS Boltzmann
solver~\cite{blas2011} (Sec.~\ref{sec:class_implementation}).

Despite absorbing CDM energy, the vacuum retains $w_\Lambda = -1$
exactly --- a property proven algebraically in
Sec.~\ref{sec:stability}.

With all five parameters determined, the model predicts two distinct
kinematic imprints on the Hubble rate: a global shift from CDM drain
and a local boost from the KBC void outflow.

\section{Kinematic Imprints: GKI and LKI}
\label{sec:gki_lki}

The five framework-derived parameters produce two distinct kinematic
signatures on the Hubble rate. The \textit{Global Kinematic Imprint}
(GKI) modifies the background expansion through CDM drain, while the
\textit{Local Kinematic Imprint} (LKI) biases local distance-ladder
measurements through the KBC void outflow. Both are zero-parameter
predictions.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------
% III.A --- GKI
% ----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Global Kinematic Imprint}
\label{sec:gki}

The CMB power spectrum fixes the physical CDM density
$\omega_{\rm cdm} \equiv \Ocdm h^2 = 0.1200 \pm 0.0012$~\cite{planck2018}
at $z \sim 1100$, where the EU coupling is strictly zero
($\eps = 0$ for $z > \ztrans$). The CMB therefore measures the same
$\omega_{\rm cdm}$ as $\Lambda$CDM.

If CDM evaporates at late times, $\Ocdm$ decreases while
$\omega_{\rm cdm}$ remains CMB-anchored. The reduced Hubble parameter
$h$ must increase to maintain the constraint:
\begin{equation}
h^{\rm EU} = \frac{h^{\rm Planck}}{\sqrt{\fsurv(0)}}\,,
\label{eq:h_gki}
\end{equation}
yielding the global prediction
\begin{equation}
\boxed{H_0^{\rm GKI}
  = \frac{H_0^{\rm Planck}}{\sqrt{\fsurv(0)}}
  = \frac{67.36}{\sqrt{0.9558}}
  \approx 68.90 \;\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}\,.}
\label{eq:H0_gki}
\end{equation}

Because CDM has evaporated, the present-day physical matter density
is $\omega_m(z{=}0) = \omega_b + \omega_{\rm cdm}\,\fsurv(0)
\approx 0.137$. The corresponding matter density parameter is
$\Omega_m^{\rm EU} = \omega_m(z{=}0) / (h^{\rm EU})^2 \approx 0.289$,
compared with $\Omega_m^{\Lambda\rm CDM} = 0.3153$.

This mechanism --- CDM drain reducing $\Ocdm$ and raising $h$ ---
is the same physics exploited by Interacting Dark Energy
models~\cite{divalentino2020}, but with a coupling
fixed by the framework ($\lambda = 2/3$) rather than
fitted to data.

\subsection{Local Kinematic Imprint}
\label{sec:lki}

The GKI predicts the \textit{global} expansion rate. Local
distance-ladder measurements are systematically higher because the
observer sits inside the KBC void~\cite{keenan2013}, a local
underdensity extending to $R \sim 300\;\text{Mpc}$ with observed
contrast $\delta_{\rm obs} = -0.46 \pm 0.06$.

The void outflow biases the locally inferred $H_0$
(Wu \& Huterer 2017~\cite{wu_huterer2017}):
\begin{equation}
\frac{\delta H_0}{H_0}
  = -\frac{1}{3}\,\delta_{\rm true}\,f(\Omega_m)\,\Theta\,,
\label{eq:void_bias}
\end{equation}
where $f(\Omega_m)$ is the linear growth rate and $\Theta$ accounts
for the non-linear correction of
Marra et al.~\cite{marra2013}.

Two refinements enter the calculation. First, the observed contrast
$\delta_{\rm obs}$ is inflated by redshift-space distortions
(Haslbauer, Banik \& Kroupa 2020~\cite{haslbauer2020}):
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\rm true}
  = \frac{\delta_{\rm obs}}{1 + f/b_g}
  = \frac{-0.46}{1 + 0.514/1.0}
  \approx -0.304\,,
\label{eq:delta_true}
\end{equation}
where $f = 0.514$ is computed from the exact Riccati
ODE~\cite{linder2005} evaluated in the EU background
(not the $\Omega_m^{0.55}$ approximation)
and $b_g = 1.0$ is the $K$-band galaxy bias. Second, the
non-linear correction $\Theta = 1.064$ amplifies the outflow by
$6.4\%$ relative to linear theory~\cite{marra2013}.

The local prediction is
\begin{align}
H_0^{\rm LKI}
  &= H_0^{\rm GKI} + \delta H_0
  \notag \\
  &= 68.90 + 3.82
  \approx \boxed{72.72\;\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}\,.}
\label{eq:H0_lki}
\end{align}

CDM drain also causes adiabatic halo expansion~\cite{gnedin2004},
but its effect on the distance ladder is negligible:
$\Delta R_{\rm halo} \sim 10\;\text{kpc}$ versus
$D_{\rm SN} \sim 40\;\text{Mpc}$ implies
$\Delta H_0 \sim 0.009\;\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$.

\textit{Sensitivity.}~The LKI prediction is robust against the
KBC void uncertainty. Propagating
$\delta_{\rm obs} = -0.46 \pm 0.06$ through the full
RSD-corrected formula yields
$H_0^{\rm LKI} \in [72.19, 73.26]$ at $1\sigma$, with worst-case
tension against SH0ES of $1.14\sigma$.

\subsection{Solver architecture}
\label{sec:solver}

Four patches are applied to the \textsc{class} source. (i)~The
header \texttt{background.h} receives the five EU parameters
($\varepsilon_{\rm IR}$, $\ztrans$, $b$, $\lambda$,
and the perturbation switch). (ii)~The parser \texttt{input.c}
reads these parameters from the configuration file, defaulting
to $\varepsilon_{\rm IR} = 0$ (standard $\Lambda$CDM).
(iii)~The background integrator \texttt{background.c} implements
the coupled ODE system
\begin{align}
\frac{d f_{\rm cdm}}{d\ln a}
  &= -\lambda\,\eps(z)\,f_{\rm cdm}\,,
\label{eq:fcdm_ode}\\[4pt]
\frac{d f_{\rm vac}}{d\ln a}
  &= +\lambda\,\eps(z)\,f_{\rm cdm}\,a^{-3}\,,
\label{eq:fvac_ode}
\end{align}
where both $f_{\rm cdm}$ and $f_{\rm vac}$ are normalized to
$\rho_{c,0} \equiv \Omega_{c,0}\,H_0^2/(8\pi G/3)$.
The system is solved by a fourth-order Runge--Kutta integrator
over 2000 steps in $\ln a \in [-7, 0]$, with initial conditions
$f_{\rm cdm} = 1$, $f_{\rm vac} = 0$.
The CDM density is multiplied by $f_{\rm cdm}(a)$ at each
background evaluation, and the vacuum receives the Friedmann
closure injection $\rho_{c,0}\,f_{\rm vac}(a)$.
(iv)~The perturbation integrator \texttt{perturbations.c}
receives the interacting dark energy (IDE) source terms
of Valiviita, Majerotto \& Maartens (2008)~\cite{valiviita2008}.

The EU and $\Lambda$CDM runs share identical cosmological
parameters (Planck 2018
baseline~\cite{planck2018}). The $\Lambda$CDM run
fixes $H_0 = 67.36\;\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$; the EU run
inputs the same $100\theta_s$ and lets \textsc{class} shoot
for~$H_0$, which is the standard procedure when comparing
models with different late-time physics.

\subsection{Perturbation stability}
\label{sec:stability}

The EU coupling $Q = -\lambda\,\eps(z)\,H\,\rhoc$ is
proportional to the local CDM density. This proportionality
guarantees algebraic cancellation of the IDE perturbation
source terms for $w_\Lambda = -1$:

\textit{Continuity.}~The source term in the CDM overdensity
equation is proportional to $[\delta Q/Q - \delta_c]$.
Since $Q \propto \rhoc$, the fractional perturbation
$\delta Q/Q = \delta_c$, yielding
$[\delta_c - \delta_c] = 0$.

\textit{Euler.}~The momentum drag is proportional to
$[\theta_{\rm de} - \theta_{\rm cdm}]$. For $w = -1$ (vacuum),
$\theta_{\rm de} = 0$; in synchronous gauge,
$\theta_{\rm cdm} = 0$ by construction. Hence
$[0 - 0] = 0$.

Dense and underdense regions evaporate at the same fractional
rate. Structure growth is modified only through the background:
the diluted Poisson source (weaker $\rho_{\rm cdm}$) produces
shallower potential wells.

\textit{Null test.}~We verify this cancellation by running
\textsc{class} twice with identical parameters but the IDE
perturbation switch toggled on and off. The difference in
$\sigma_8$ is
\begin{align}
\Delta\sigma_8
  &= |\sigma_8^{\rm IDE\;on} - \sigma_8^{\rm IDE\;off}|
  \notag \\
  &= 0\quad (\text{exact to machine precision})\,.
\label{eq:null_test}
\end{align}

\textit{Doom factor.}~Standard IDE models with $w \neq -1$
suffer from large-scale instabilities when the ratio
$Q/[(1+w)\rho_{\rm de}]$ diverges~\cite{gavela2009,valiviita2008}.
In the EU, $w_\Lambda = -1$ exactly: the vacuum does not
cluster ($\delta_{\rm de} = 0$) and carries no velocity
perturbations ($\theta_{\rm de} = 0$). The dark energy
Euler equation drops out of the perturbation system,
so the singular ratio never arises. Unlike standard IDE
models where $w$ is a free parameter that must be tuned
away from $-1$ to avoid this instability, the EU derives
$w_\Lambda = -1$ algebraically from the Cancellation
Theorem. The doom factor instability is thus structurally
absent --- a consequence of the model's internal
consistency, not a numerical choice.

\subsection{Sampling strategy}
\label{sec:methodology}

The analysis comprises six MCMC runs organized in three
phases across two parameter configurations.

The first configuration, \textit{EU-blind} ($\Delta k = 3$),
samples the three EU parameters
($\varepsilon_{\rm IR}$, $\ztrans$, $b$) with flat priors
alongside the standard cosmological parameters. The second,
\textit{EU} ($\Delta k = 0$), fixes all EU parameters at
the framework-derived values of Sec.~\ref{sec:params}.

\textit{Phase~1} runs each configuration with the IDE
perturbation source terms disabled, generating stable
proposal covariance matrices.
\textit{Phase~2} activates the source terms and uses the
Phase~1 covariance as the proposal, producing the baseline
posteriors.
\textit{Phase~3} extends the EU configuration with local
measurements:
\textit{EU+$H_0$} adds the SH0ES Cepheid distance-ladder
measurement~\cite{breuval2024};
\textit{EU+$H_0$+$S_8$} adds DES-Y3
$3 \times 2$pt cosmic shear via
Cocoa/CosmoLike~\cite{des2022,krause2021}, sampling 23
additional nuisance parameters (photometric redshifts,
shear calibration, intrinsic alignments, and galaxy bias).

All six runs share a baseline likelihood consisting of
Planck PR4 NPIPE CamSpec TTTEEE, low-$\ell$ TT and EE,
CMB lensing
$\phi\phi$~\cite{planck2020npipe}, DESI BAO
DR2~\cite{desi_dr2_2025}, and Pantheon+ SNe
Ia~\cite{scolnic2022}. Both the EU and $\Lambda$CDM
sample the same seven parameters: the six
$\Lambda$CDM cosmological parameters plus the Planck
calibration nuisance $A_{\rm planck}$.
Convergence is assessed via the Gelman--Rubin
statistic~\cite{gelman1992}; all six runs satisfy
$R-1 < 0.01$.

\subsection{EU parameters: free versus derived}
\label{sec:posteriors}

Table~\ref{tab:mcmc} presents the full posterior constraints.
The $\Lambda$CDM column reports the best-fit from a Cobaya
minimizer (BOBYQA, best of 4 starts) applied to the baseline

The comparison between the EU-blind and EU columns of

of this analysis.

\textit{EU-blind $\to$ EU.}~The EU-blind run tests whether the data actively prefer evaporation.
The posterior $\varepsilon_{\rm IR} = 0.075 \pm 0.043$
excludes zero drain and is consistent with the
framework-derived value at $0.75\sigma$. However, the three
EU parameters are not individually constrained. Flat priors
on ($\varepsilon_{\rm IR}$, $z_{\rm trans}$, $b$) create a
three-dimensional degeneracy: geometrically distinct
combinations of transition redshift and coupling strength
produce nearly identical background expansion histories.
Current CMB, BAO, and SNe data constrain the integrated
drain fraction $f_{\rm cdm}(z{=}0)$ but lack the redshift
resolution to separate $\varepsilon_{\rm IR}$,

${>}\,47\%$; $f_{\rm cdm} = 0.921 \pm 0.053$).

volume of combinations that inflate the CDM drain beyond
its framework-derived value.
To preserve the acoustic peak structure under excess
evaporation, the MCMC compensates by lowering
$\omega_{\rm cdm}$ to $0.1178$ --- a $3.6\sigma$

which suppresses $H_0^{\rm GKI}$ to
$68.13\;\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$.

Fixing the three EU parameters at their framework-derived
values eliminates this degeneracy. A single evaporation
curve --- a drain of exactly $4.42\%$ of CDM since

$0.76\;\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$ to $68.89$. The best-fit $\chi^2$
increases by only $1.9$ for three fewer degrees of freedom;
the Akaike information criterion accordingly favors the
theory-fixed configuration
($\Delta\text{AIC} = -4.1$; see
Appendix~\ref{app:free_params}).

\subsection{Model selection}
\label{sec:model_selection}

With identical parameter counts ($k_{\rm EU} = k_{\Lambda\rm CDM}
= 7$), the information criteria~\cite{burnham_anderson2002}
reduce to pure goodness-of-fit:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\text{AIC} = \Delta\text{BIC}
  = \Delta\chi^2\,.
\label{eq:aic}
\end{equation}
This is the cleanest possible comparison: no Occam penalty,
no prior-volume ambiguity. Two dataset configurations are
compared, both using the $\Lambda$CDM best-fit obtained via
Cobaya minimizer (BOBYQA) on identical datasets. The
baseline comprises CMB, BAO, and SNe; the extended
configuration adds the SH0ES distance-ladder measurement
under symmetric conditions --- both models receive the KBC
void outflow correction of
Wu \& Huterer~\cite{wu_huterer2017}:
\begin{align}
\Delta\text{AIC}_{\rm baseline}
  &= -3.09 \quad (\text{positive evidence})\,,
  \label{eq:aic_baseline} \\
\Delta\text{AIC}_{\rm baseline+SH0ES}
  &= -6.88 \quad (\text{strong evidence})\,.
  \label{eq:aic_shoes}
\end{align}
The baseline improvement is driven by CMB
($\Delta\chi^2_{\rm CMB} = -4.2$) and BAO
($\Delta\chi^2_{\rm BAO} = -2.6$), partially offset by SNe
($\Delta\chi^2_{\rm SNe} = +3.7$). Adding SH0ES
consolidates the advantage: the EU predicts the correct
local $H_0$ from geometry alone, converting what is a
$5\sigma$ tension for $\Lambda$CDM into a
$0.5\sigma$ agreement.

The DES-Y3 data are not included in the model
selection. The published
$S_8 = 0.776$~\cite{des2022} was obtained under
$\Lambda$CDM using DES data alone, while the EU posterior
uses five combined datasets; a fair $\chi^2$ comparison
requires both models to be evaluated on identical data,
which was not performed for the DES-Y3 component.

The parameter shifts documented in
Table~\ref{tab:mcmc} --- primordial parameters unchanged,
acoustic densities rebalanced, sound horizon angle shifted
--- constitute a characteristic three-layer signature of the
CDM drain. The physical interpretation is developed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:drain}.

\textit{Layer~2 --- Acoustic.}~The baryon density

loading that governs the odd-even peak asymmetry in the
CMB power spectrum~\cite{hu_dodelson2002}; under the EU
expansion history, the MCMC rebalances $\omega_b$ to
preserve the peak structure measured by CamSpec.
The resulting $\omega_{\rm cdm} = 0.1193$ is the
primordial CDM density at recombination
($z \sim 1100$). The effective density today, after
$4.4\%$ evaporation, is
\begin{equation}
\omega_{\rm cdm}^{\rm eff}(0) =
  \omega_{\rm cdm}\, f_{\rm cdm}(0)
  = 0.1193 \cdot 0.9558 = 0.1140\,.
\label{eq:omega_eff}
\end{equation}
This $4.4\%$ reduction relative to the primordial
value is the origin of the reduced $\Omega_m$ and the
suppressed structure growth that alleviates the $S_8$
tension.

\textit{Layer~3 --- Geometric ($+3.6\sigma$).}~The
sound-horizon angle
$100\theta_s = r_s(z_*) / D_A(z_*)$ shifts from
$1.04097$ ($\Lambda$CDM) to $1.04180$ (EU). The CDM drain
transfers energy to the vacuum sector, accelerating the
late-time expansion and reducing $D_A(z_*)$. At fixed
sound horizon, the reduced angular diameter distance
requires a higher $H_0$ to preserve the observed CMB
peak positions --- yielding
$H_0^{\rm GKI} = 68.89\;\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$ instead of
$67.82$.

The hierarchy --- primordial frozen, densities rebalanced,
geometry shifted --- is the expected signature of a
late-time mechanism that modifies the expansion history
while leaving recombination physics intact.

\subsection{Two-scale resolution}
\label{sec:two_scale}

The EU respects the geometric wall rather than
attempting to breach it. The resolution separates
two physically distinct scales: a global kinematic
imprint (GKI) from the CDM drain, and a local
kinematic imprint (LKI) from the KBC void outflow
(Fig.~\ref{fig:wall_escape}(b)).

The GKI modifies the global Friedmann equation.
The CDM drain reduces $\Omega_{\rm cdm}$ at
$z < \ztrans$, and flatness requires $H_0$ to
increase to compensate. The framework predicts
$H_0^{\rm GKI} = 68.90$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:gki_lki});
the MCMC posteriors confirm
$H_0^{\rm GKI} = 68.89 \pm 0.28\;\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:posteriors}).
This shift is anchored by the CMB:
$\omega_{\rm cdm}$ is fixed at recombination, and the
drain fraction $f_{\rm cdm}(0) = 0.9558$ is determined
by the framework parameters. The GKI operates on the
entire observable universe and affects all cosmological
probes uniformly. At $H_0^{\rm GKI} = 68.90$, the
EU sits within the parabolic minimum of the profile
likelihood --- well below the geometric wall.

The LKI closes the remaining gap. The KBC void
($\delta_{\rm obs} = -0.46 \pm 0.06$, $R \approx
300\;\text{Mpc}$~\cite{keenan2013}) is an observed
underdensity surrounding the Milky Way. Within such a
void, bulk outflow biases distance-ladder measurements of
$H_0$~\cite{wu_huterer2017}. The magnitude of the bias
depends on the growth rate: $\delta H_0 / H_0 =
-(1/3)\,\delta_{\rm true}\,f(\Omega_m)$, where
$\delta_{\rm true} = -0.304$ after RSD
correction~\cite{haslbauer2020}. In the EU, the
growth rate $f(\Omega_m)$ is computed with the
self-consistent $\Omega_m = 0.288$, yielding
$\delta H_0 = +3.82\;\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$.
The void bias depends on $f(\Omega_m)$, which is
itself modified by the CDM drain: the same physics
that produces the GKI also determines the magnitude
of the LKI. The local boost is not an external
correction appended to the model --- it is a
consequence of the drain operating through structure
growth.

The two channels are additive and independent:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
H_0^{\rm LKI} &= H_0^{\rm GKI} + \delta H_0^{\rm void} \\
&= 68.90 + 3.82
= 72.72\;\text{km}\,\text{s}^{-1}\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}\,.
\end{split}
\label{eq:h0_lki}
\end{equation}
The GKI modifies the background cosmology; the LKI
modifies only the local frame of the observer. This
separation is rigid: the void outflow does not enter
the Friedmann equation and does not affect the CMB,
BAO, or any other global probe. It biases exclusively
the distance-ladder measurements that calibrate
supernovae within $\sim\!300\;\text{Mpc}$.

All local calibration methods --- Cepheids (SH0ES),
TRGB, JAGB, Miras --- share a common Rung~3
(supernovae in the Hubble flow) and therefore share
the void bias. The EU predicts that these methods
should cluster near $H_0^{\rm LKI} \approx 72.7$,
while CMB and inverse-distance-ladder measurements
should cluster near $H_0^{\rm GKI} \approx 68.9$. The
observed hierarchy (Table~\ref{tab:hierarchy}) is
consistent with this prediction
(Sec.~\ref{sec:hierarchy}).

measurements. The acoustic scale is determined at
recombination, where the EU and $\Lambda$CDM are

subtle but detectable modification of the growth
rate.

Pantheon+ compilation~\cite{brout2022} contributes
$\chi^2 \approx 1408$ for both models --- the CDM drain
is too weak ($\varepsilon_{\rm IR} = 0.0426$) to produce
a measurable shift in distance moduli. The 32-point
cosmic-chronometer compilation~\cite{moresco2022}
yields $\Delta\chi^2 < 1$. Both datasets confirm that
the EU expansion history is conservative: it modifies
$H(z)$ at the percent level, preserving the
calibrated distance ladder.

We use Gadget-4~\cite{gadget4} with a custom EU patch that

Both simulations use $1024^3$ particles in a periodic box

to machine precision ($\sim\!10^{-14}\%$). This
interpolator feeds the downstream lensing analysis
(Sec.~\ref{sec:growth}) and the Stage~IV survey
predictions (Sec.~\ref{sec:predictions}).

\section{Resolution of the Growth Tension}
\label{sec:growth}

The parameter $S_8 \equiv \sigma_8\sqrt{\Omega_m/0.3}$ reported

link in this inference chain carries a systematic bias.

arguments. The EU+$H_0$+$S_8$ run (Sec.~\ref{sec:mcmc_confirmation})
proves numerically that DES-Y3 data are consistent with
$S_8 = 0.815$ when analyzed under EU cosmology. The forward
analysis explains the mechanism: geometric kernel
bias of $-5\%$ to $-7\%$ systematically depresses the
$S_8$ inferred by $\Lambda$CDM pipelines.

\subsection{The lensing kernel bias}
\label{sec:kernel_bias}

Weak-lensing surveys measure the angular cross-spectrum of
cosmic shear between tomographic bins $i$ and $j$. Under the
Limber approximation~\cite{limber1953},
\begin{equation}
C_\ell^{ij} = \int_0^{\chi_H}
  \frac{W_i(\chi)\,W_j(\chi)}{\chi^2}\;
  P\!\left(\frac{\ell + 1/2}{\chi},\, z(\chi)\right)
  d\chi\,,
\label{eq:limber}
\end{equation}
where $P(k,z)$ is the matter power spectrum and $W_i(\chi)$
is the lensing efficiency kernel for bin $i$:
\begin{equation}
W_i(\chi) = \frac{3\,\Omega_m\,H_0^2}{2\,c^2}\;
  \frac{\chi}{a(\chi)}\;
  \int_\chi^{\chi_H}
  n_i(\chi')\,\frac{\chi' - \chi}{\chi'}\;d\chi'\,.
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
Three quantities in Eq.~(\ref{eq:kernel}) depend on the
assumed cosmology: the matter density $\Omega_m$, the
comoving distance $\chi(z)$, and the source distribution
$n_i(\chi)$ mapped from redshift space. The EU modifications
--- $\Omega_m^{\rm EU} = 0.288$ (vs $0.315$) and
$\chi_{\rm EU}(z) \neq \chi_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(z)$ due to
the altered expansion history --- shift the kernel peaks,
change the overlap between tomographic bins, and modify
the Limber mapping $k = (\ell + 1/2)/\chi$.

A $\Lambda$CDM pipeline analyzing shear data from an EU
universe uses the wrong kernel in every integral. To
quantify the resulting bias, we define the
\textit{pipeline-inferred} value
\begin{equation}
\hat{S}_8 = S_8^{\rm EU}\,
  \sqrt{\left\langle A_{ij} \right\rangle_{\rm Fisher}}\,,
\label{eq:shat}
\end{equation}
where $A_{ij} = C_\ell^{ij}(\text{EU}) /
C_\ell^{ij}(\Lambda\text{CDM})$ is the amplitude ratio
of the true (EU) to template ($\Lambda$CDM) angular spectra,
and the Fisher average weights each bin pair by its
signal-to-noise ratio. The ratio $\hat{S}_8 / S_8^{\rm EU}$
is the kernel bias.

\textit{UV extrapolation.}~The Gadget-4 simulation has

The tension with DES-Y3 increases from $1.03\sigma$ to

independently confirms $S_8 = 0.815 \pm 0.007$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:mcmc_confirmation}). The forward
analysis is a diagnostic tool that explains the
mechanism; the full MCMC is the definitive numerical

photo-$z$ marginalization, shear calibration, and the
complete off-diagonal covariance matrix.

The EU growth tension is best quantified through the

CMB, BAO, and supernovae simultaneously. The third is
the full posterior, $S_8 = 0.815 \pm 0.007$, which

$S_8 = 0.811$ without seeing any shear data, and the

$\Lambda$CDM, the CMB predicts $S_8 = 0.832$, while

\subsection{Euclid DR1 weak lensing and spectroscopy}
\label{sec:euclid}

\textit{Anomalous $C_\ell$ pattern.}~The lensing kernel bias
identified in Sec.~\ref{sec:kernel_bias} predicts a specific
suppression of the angular power spectrum measurable by
Euclid~\cite{euclid2024}. Across the 10 tomographic bins of the
Euclid photometric survey ($0.001 < z < 2.5$), the lensing
kernel $W(\chi)$ is suppressed by $-8.1\%$ (Bin~1,
$\bar{z} = 0.30$) to $-4.7\%$ (Bin~10, $\bar{z} = 1.85$). 
Since $C_\ell \propto W^2$, the power spectrum deficit ranges
from approximately $-16\%$ at low redshift to $-9\%$ at high
redshift.

This pattern has four distinguishing properties:
(i)~negative at all redshifts, (ii)~monotonically decreasing
with increasing $z$ (the CDM drain is strongest at late
times), (iii)~smooth in $\ell$ (no oscillatory features),
and (iv)~strictly zero in B-modes. No known $\Lambda$CDM
systematic produces this combination. Photometric redshift
errors generate oscillatory residuals in $\ell$; shear
calibration errors are redshift-independent; intrinsic
alignments contaminate B-modes. The EU signature is
geometrically distinct from all three.

\textit{Spectral crossing.}~A further discriminant emerges from the
scale dependence of the $C_\ell$ ratio. At low multipoles
($\ell \lesssim 255$), the geometric kernel bias
dominates: the EU lensing kernels are ${\sim}\,6\%$ weaker
than their $\Lambda$CDM counterparts, suppressing
$C_\ell^{\rm EU}/C_\ell^{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}$ below unity.
At high multipoles ($\ell \gtrsim 255$), the EU matter
power spectrum amplitude takes over: $\sigma_8^{\rm EU} =
0.827$ exceeds $\sigma_8^{\Lambda{\rm CDM}} = 0.811$,
injecting more small-scale power and driving the ratio
above unity. The crossover at $\ell \approx 255$ is a
shape-dependent prediction that does not depend on
the overall normalization of $C_\ell$ --- it depends on
the interplay between a geometric effect (kernel) and
a dynamical effect ($P(k)$ amplitude). Stage-III surveys,
with $\ell_{\rm max} \lesssim 300$, probe only the
suppressed regime. Euclid, reaching $\ell \sim 5000$
across 10 tomographic bins, will map both regimes and
measure the crossover scale directly.

\textit{Pipeline-inferred $\hat{S}_8$.}~The Euclid CUB prediction for the
pipeline-inferred parameter is $\hat{S}_8 = 0.801$. With
Euclid's projected uncertainty $\sigma(S_8) \approx 0.01$
and the Planck uncertainty $\sigma = 0.013$ combined in
quadrature, this lies ${\sim}\,1.9\sigma$ below the Planck
$\Lambda$CDM value ($S_8 = 0.832$). The EU predicts that
this persistent deficit will be interpreted as a growth
tension within $\Lambda$CDM --- but will resolve to
$< 1\sigma$ when the EU lensing kernel is adopted.

\textit{Alcock--Paczy\'nski anomaly.}~The EU expansion
history modifies the radial and transverse distance
scales measured by Euclid's spectroscopic survey
(NISP grism, $0.9 < z < 1.8$). The Alcock--Paczy\'nski
parameters~\cite{alcock_paczynski1979} deviate from
unity:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_\parallel(z{=}1) = 1.012\,,\qquad
\alpha_\perp(z{=}1) = 0.998\,.
\label{eq:ap}
\end{equation}
The radial stretching ($\alpha_\parallel > 1$ by $1.2\%$)
arises because $H_{\rm EU}(z) \neq H_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(z)$;
the transverse parameter $\alpha_\perp$ exhibits a sign
change at $z \approx 1.2$, coinciding with the crossover
in the Limber mapping shift
$\Delta k/k = (\chi_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}} - \chi_{\rm EU})/
\chi_{\rm EU}$.
This test is purely spectroscopic and shares no nuisance
parameters with the photometric $3 \times 2$pt analysis.

\textit{$3 \times 2$pt probe decomposition.}~The remaining
legs of the $3 \times 2$pt analysis carry
independent EU signatures. The galaxy clustering kernel
$q(\chi) = b(z)\,n(z)\,H(z)/c$ is biased by
$\Delta H/H$, which ranges from $+0.7\%$ at $z = 0.3$
to $-1.7\%$ at $z = 1.85$ --- changing sign at
$z \approx 0.5$ as the GKI boost
(Sec.~\ref{sec:gki}) gives way to the freezing of the
drain at high redshift. This bias varies
\textit{within} each tomographic bin: $H(z)$ is not
constant across the bin width, so the per-bin galaxy
bias $b_i$ (a free nuisance parameter, constant within
each bin) cannot absorb the signal. The mismatch between
the intra-bin $H(z)$ shape in EU versus $\Lambda$CDM is
a non-degenerate signature.

Galaxy-galaxy lensing $C_\ell^{\delta\gamma}$ inherits
bias from all three physical sources simultaneously:
the lensing kernel deficit ($-5\%$ to $-8\%$), the
clustering kernel shift ($\pm\!2\%$), and the Limber
mapping displacement ($\pm\!1\%$). Their combination
produces a total bias of $-6.0\%$ at $z = 0.3$ to
$-7.1\%$ at $z = 0.96$ --- making galaxy-galaxy lensing
the single most sensitive Euclid observable to the EU
modification. This sensitivity arises because the cross-
correlation probes both the matter distribution (lensing)
and the galaxy field (clustering) through kernels that
are \textit{both} shifted by the altered expansion
history.

The nuisance-free statistic $E_G$~\cite{zhang2007},
defined as the ratio of lensing-galaxy to galaxy-galaxy
angular spectra, cancels galaxy bias $b$ algebraically.
In $\Lambda$CDM, $E_G = \Omega_m/f(z)$, where $f(z)$ is
the linear growth rate. In the EU, both $\Omega_m(z)$ and
$f(z)$ are modified by the drain. The resulting bias
ranges from $-4.7\%$ at $z = 0.3$ (where the CDM deficit
is largest) to $-0.02\%$ at $z = 1.85$ (where the drain
freezes and the EU converges to $\Lambda$CDM). This
convergence is itself a prediction: $E_G(z)$ should
approach the $\Lambda$CDM value at $z > z_{\rm trans}$,
providing a direct measurement of the evaporation onset
redshift.

is weaker) than in shallow bins (where it is stronger).

inconsistency when analyzed under $\Lambda$CDM. The EU
predicts that it will be attributed to photo-$z$
systematic errors at high redshift --- but will resolve

\textit{BAO distance residuals.}~At low redshift ($z \lesssim 0.5$),

A useful organizing principle is the
\textit{derivation hierarchy} of model parameters:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Level~0 (parametrization):}
  The functional form is postulated without physical
  motivation. The CPL parametrization
  $w(a) = w_0 + w_a(1-a)$ is the standard example.

\item \textbf{Level~1 (phenomenological):}
  A coupling form is postulated
  (e.g., $Q = \xi H\rhoc$ or $Q = \xi H\rho_{\rm de}$)
  with numerical coefficients fitted to data. This
  describes the IDE models of
  Di~Valentino et al.~\cite{divalentino2020},
  decaying dark matter~\cite{vattis2019},
  and $\Lambda_s$CDM~\cite{akarsu2023}.

\item \textbf{Level~2 (form derived, values fitted):}
  The coupling form is motivated by quantum field theory,
  but numerical parameters remain free.
  EDE~\cite{poulin2019_ede} derives an axion-like potential
  from the string landscape; the Running Vacuum
  Model~\cite{sola2017_rvm} derives
  $\Lambda(H) = c_0 + \nu H^2$ from QFT in curved
  spacetime. In both cases, at least one coefficient
  ($f_{\rm EDE}$, $z_c$, or $\nu$) is fitted by MCMC.

\item \textbf{Level~3 (form and values derived):}
  Both the functional form and all numerical parameters
  are determined by the theoretical framework, with
  $\Delta k = 0$. The EU is the only published model
  in this category.
\end{itemize}

The closest competitor in derivation rigor is the
Running Vacuum Model of Sol\`a
Peracaula~\cite{sola2017_rvm}. Both the RVM and the EU
invoke QFT to modify the dark sector; both produce
a coupling of the form $Q \propto H\rhoc$. The
difference is surgical: the RVM running coefficient
$\nu$ is \textit{motivated} by renormalization group
arguments but \textit{fitted} by MCMC, placing it at
Level~2. The EU coupling
$\lambda\varepsilon_{\rm IR} = (2/3)({\pi}/{4})/\sqrt{108\pi}$
is \textit{calculated} from the Gribov--Zwanziger
effective action and \textit{confirmed} by MCMC
(Sec.~\ref{sec:concordance}), placing it at Level~3.

Phenomenological IDE models ($Q = \xi H\rhoc$)
and decaying dark matter~\cite{vattis2019}
are the Level~1 counterparts of the EU:
they postulate the same coupling form that the EU
derives. The EU thus \textit{provides the microscopic
mechanism} that the IDE literature assumes.

The DESI DR2 measurement of $w_0 > -1$ at
${\sim}\,2.8\sigma$~\cite{desi_dr2_2025} is often
interpreted as evidence for dynamical dark energy.

$w_\Lambda = -1$ exactly (Sec.~\ref{sec:stability}),

EU-generated distances recovers $w_0 = -1.006$
when $\Omega_m$ is free (the EU \textit{is} a
cosmological constant) and $w_0 = -0.855$ when
$\Omega_m$ is fixed to the Planck CMB prior
(Sec.~\ref{sec:cpl_mirage}). The latter lies
$0.3\sigma$ from the DESI central value
($w_0 = -0.838 \pm 0.055$), confirming that
the DESI signal is quantitatively consistent with
a CDM drain artifact.

\subsection{Theoretical structure and limitations}
\label{sec:theory_limits}

The EU rests on a physical distinction between the 4D
spacetime bulk and the 3D spatial hypersurface $\Sigma$.
While the ADM decomposition is conventionally treated as
a gauge choice, the Gribov--Zwanziger ghost
condensate~\cite{gribov1978,zwanziger1989,vandersickel2012}
is a non-perturbative phenomenon defined on the 4D
manifold that affects $\Sigma$ through a calculable
barrier. The 10 independent components of $g_{\mu\nu}$
and the six independent components of $\gamma_{ij}$ define
two physically distinct domains; the four constrained
degrees of freedom (lapse and shift, removed by the
Hamiltonian and momentum constraints) constitute the
interface between them. The requirement that all vacuum
fluctuations respect spherical isotropy --- the same
geometric principle that fixes the factor $4\pi$ in
Gauss's law --- recurs at every stage of the EU
derivation: in the heat-kernel prefactor
$(4\pi)^{-d/2}|_{d=4}$ that sets $z_{\rm trans}$,
in the loop measure $(4\pi)^{-1}$ that enters
$\varepsilon_{\rm bare}$, and in the CKN volume
$(4\pi/3)R_H^3$ that establishes the UV cutoff.
This geometric coherence is the reason
$\Delta k = 0$: no free parameters survive when
isotropy fully determines the structure at every scale.

The triple convergence on $\lambda = 2/3$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:lambda}) and the Slavnov--Taylor
protection of this value to all orders
(Eq.~\ref{eq:ward}) ensure that the effective coupling
$\varepsilon_{\rm IR} = (\pi/4)\,\varepsilon_{\rm bare}$
contains zero adjustable coefficients.

\textit{Why baryons are immune.}~The democratic coupling
derived in Sec.~\ref{sec:eps} requires the interacting
field to be a gauge singlet under the Ashtekar SU(2)
connection: only singlet states satisfy the
Wigner--Eckart condition for uniform coupling to all
27 ghost channels. Baryonic matter carries Standard
Model gauge charges
($\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\rm QCD} \times \mathrm{U}(1)_{\rm EM}$)
and transforms non-trivially under the full gauge group.
Its internal quantum numbers break the democratic
coupling condition, excluding it from the condensate
interaction. This selection rule is structural: it is
the negative corollary of the same representation
theory that determines the CDM coupling amplitude
$\varepsilon_{\rm bare}$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:epsbare}).

\textit{Limitations.}~Several aspects of the present
analysis require qualification.
(i)~The growth tension is resolved to
$\sim\!1\sigma$ using the CUB forward model
(Sec.~\ref{sec:kernel_bias}), but the baryonic
feedback correction relies on the ratio
$P_{\rm EU}/P_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}$ from a
gravity-only $N$-body simulation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:nbody}). A hydrodynamic simulation
incorporating the CDM drain alongside baryonic
processes would sharpen this estimate.
(ii)~The local kinematic imprint (LKI) accounts for
$\sim\!70\%$ of the $H_0$ resolution.
The KBC void parameters ($\delta \approx -0.30$,
$R \sim 300$~Mpc) are observational inputs, not
EU-specific predictions; the EU permits and
quantifies the void boost, but does not create
the void.
(iii)~The framework has been validated with a single
Boltzmann code implementation (CLASS-EU).
Independent reproduction --- using, e.g., CAMB with
equivalent patches --- is a natural next step
following publication. The full MCMC infrastructure
(CLASS patches, Cobaya configuration files,
Gadget-4 scripts) will be made publicly available
to facilitate this.
(iv)~The CDM drain modifies $H(z)$ at the percent
level, by design. Differential tests therefore
require high-precision data; the predictions of
Sec.~\ref{sec:predictions} are calibrated to the
sensitivity of Euclid, LSST, and DESI DR3.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The Evaporating Universe is an interacting dark energy model
in which a Gribov--Zwanziger ghost condensate mediates a
continuous transfer of energy from CDM to the vacuum. All
five model parameters are derived from EFT in curved
spacetime; none are fitted to data ($\Delta k = 0$).
The principal findings are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textit{Framework.}~The coupling
$Q = -\lambda\,\varepsilon(z)\,H\,\rhoc$ is the unique
leading-order interaction consistent with FLRW symmetry
and Fermi's golden rule. The dissipation fraction
$\lambda = 2/3$, logistic exponent $b = 19/36$, transition
redshift $z_{\rm trans} \approx 5.986$, bare amplitude
$\varepsilon_{\rm bare} \approx 0.0543$, and effective
coupling $\varepsilon_{\rm IR} \approx 0.0426$ are
determined by the spatial dimensionality $n = 3$ and the
topological structure of the Gribov horizon. The
Cancellation Theorem guarantees $w_\Lambda = -1$ exactly,
eliminating the doom factor instability that plagues
generic IDE models.

\item \textit{Hubble tension.}~The CDM drain raises the
global expansion rate to
$H_0^{\rm GKI} = 68.89 \pm 0.28$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$.
The KBC void outflow adds a local kinematic imprint,
yielding
$H_0^{\rm LKI} = 72.71 \pm 0.30$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$
--- within $0.52\sigma$ of
SH0ES~\cite{breuval2024}. Adding the SH0ES likelihood
to the MCMC shifts the posterior by $0.25\sigma$,
confirming that the local measurement is redundant
rather than constraining.

\item \textit{Growth tension.}~Weak-lensing surveys
infer $S_8$ using a $\Lambda$CDM lensing kernel that
systematically underestimates the amplitude in an
evaporating universe. Forward-modeling the EU kernel
yields $\hat{S}_8 = 0.794$ for
DES-Y3~\cite{des2022}, reducing the tension from
${\sim}\,2.6\sigma$ to $1.03\sigma$ (kernel-corrected).
Including DES-Y3 data directly in the MCMC yields
$S_8 = 0.815 \pm 0.007$ --- within $0.58\sigma$ of the
blind EU prediction ($0.811$).

\item \textit{Validation.}~All six MCMC runs converge at
$R-1 < 0.01$, including the 38-dimensional
EU+$H_0$+$S_8$ configuration. The UV-derived
parameters match the Phase~2 EU posteriors (CMB+BAO+SNe)
to $0.04\sigma$~(Sec.~\ref{sec:concordance}).
A Gadget-4 $N$-body simulation ($1024^3$ particles)
confirms the drain signature in $P(k)$.
Model selection yields
$\Delta\text{AIC} = -6.88$ in the fair-fight
configuration ($\Delta k = 0$, both models
void-corrected).

\item \textit{Falsifiability.}~Ten zero-parameter
predictions target Euclid DR1, LSST Y1, and DESI DR3.
Prediction P6 --- the CPL mirage
($w_0 = -0.855$ when $\Omega_m$ is fixed to the CMB
prior) --- already matches the DESI DR2 measurement
($w_0 = -0.838 \pm 0.055$) at $0.3\sigma$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:cpl_mirage}). The remaining nine span
$C_\ell$ suppression ($-9\%$ to $-16\%$),
$\hat{S}_8 = 0.801$ ($1.9\sigma$ below Planck under
$\Lambda$CDM),
Alcock--Paczy\'nski stretching
($\alpha_\parallel = 1.012$ at $z = 1$),
LSST kernel bias gradient
($-7.9\%$ to $-4.8\%$ across five bins), and
BAO distance excess at $z > 2$.
If any prediction fails, the framework is
falsified --- because all derive from the same
modified $H(z)$.

\end{enumerate}

The Evaporating Universe resolves both cosmological
tensions simultaneously, with zero additional free
parameters --- not by fitting the data, but by
predicting it.

\subsection{Ghost-graviton vertex and TT projection}
\label{app:tt_vertex}

The ghost-graviton vertex in $d = 4$ linearized quantum gravity
reads~\cite{codello2009}
\begin{align}
V^\mu_{\;\nu;\rho\sigma}(p,q) = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[
  &\delta^\mu_\rho\, q_\sigma\, p_\nu
  + \delta^\mu_\sigma\, q_\rho\, p_\nu
  + \delta_{\nu\rho}\, p_\sigma\, q^\mu
  \notag \\
  &+ \delta_{\nu\sigma}\, p_\rho\, q^\mu
  - \delta_{\rho\sigma}(q^\mu p_\nu + p^\mu q_\nu)
  \notag \\
  &- \delta^\mu_\nu(p_\rho q_\sigma + p_\sigma q_\rho
  - \delta_{\rho\sigma}\,p{\cdot}q)\big],
\label{eq:vertex}
\end{align}
where $p$ is the ghost momentum and $q = p + k$ the anti-ghost
momentum.

The York TT projector on symmetric rank-2
tensors~\cite{york1973} is
\begin{equation}
\Pi^{\rm TT}_{\rho\sigma,\lambda\tau}(k) =
  \tfrac{1}{2}(P_{\rho\lambda}P_{\sigma\tau}
  + P_{\rho\tau}P_{\sigma\lambda})
  - \tfrac{1}{d{-}1}P_{\rho\sigma}P_{\lambda\tau},
\label{eq:projector}
\end{equation}
where $P_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu} - k_\mu k_\nu/k^2$ is the
transverse projector, satisfying idempotency
($\Pi^{\rm TT}\cdot\Pi^{\rm TT} = \Pi^{\rm TT}$), transversality
($k^\rho\Pi^{\rm TT}_{\rho\sigma,\lambda\tau} = 0$), and
tracelessness
($\delta^{\rho\sigma}\Pi^{\rm TT}_{\rho\sigma,\lambda\tau} = 0$).

The TT trace ratio
$b = {\rm Tr}(V{\cdot}\Pi^{\rm TT}{\cdot}V)/
     {\rm Tr}(V{\cdot}\mathbb{I}{\cdot}V)$
is evaluated using irreducible exact rational arithmetic, ensuring
zero floating-point contamination. Four independent momentum
configurations yield:

\begin{table}[pos=!htbp]
\caption{Verification of $b = 19/36$ across four momentum
  configurations. All computations use exact rational arithmetic.}
\label{tab:b_verification}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
Configuration & ${\rm Tr}_{\rm TT}/p^2$ &
  ${\rm Tr}_{\rm full}/p^2$ & Ratio \\
\hline
$p \perp k$, axes (1,2) & $19/3$ & $12$ & $19/36$ \\
$p \perp k$, axes (2,3) & $19/3$ & $12$ & $19/36$ \\
$p \perp k$, axes (4,1) & $19/3$ & $12$ & $19/36$ \\
$|p|{=}|k|{=}\sqrt{2}$  & $38/3$ & $24$ & $19/36$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent
The ratio is momentum-independent, as required for a well-defined
projector fraction. The identity on symmetric tensors entering the
denominator is
$\mathbb{I}_{\rho\sigma,\lambda\tau} =
  \frac{1}{2}(\delta_{\rho\lambda}\delta_{\sigma\tau}
  + \delta_{\rho\tau}\delta_{\sigma\lambda})$.

\subsection{Gribov propagator integral}
\label{app:gribov_integral}

The screening ratio $I_{\rm IR}/I_{\rm UV}$ is evaluated on the
two-dimensional transverse momentum plane. This requires
justification beyond the channel counting of
Sec.~\ref{sec:eps}: eliminating the longitudinal
polarization ($\lambda = 2/3$) reduces the number of active
\textit{channels} but does not by itself reduce the dimensionality of
the momentum integral --- a photon has two polarizations yet
integrates over $d^3k$.

The 2D measure follows from Zwanziger's horizon dominance
theorem~\cite{zwanziger1991}: in the thermodynamic limit
$V \to \infty$, the functional measure concentrates on the Gribov
horizon $\partial\Omega$ --- a codimension-1 surface in configuration
space --- for the same reason that the volume of a high-dimensional
sphere concentrates at its surface. The CDM$\,\to\,$vacuum transition
crosses this boundary (Sec.~\ref{sec:gz}), so by Fermi's
golden rule the density of accessible final states is defined on
$\partial\Omega$, yielding the transverse measure $k\,dk/(2\pi)$.

An independent route converges on the same result: the CKN
holographic bound (Sec.~\ref{sec:ztrans}, Link~2) establishes that
the effective degrees of freedom in a causal volume are governed by
its bounding surface, not its bulk. Both the Zwanziger argument
(field-space boundary) and the CKN argument (geometric boundary)
select the 2D measure.

\textit{UV reference} (free massive propagator,
$D_{\rm UV} = 1/(k^2 + \gamma^2)$):
\begin{equation}
I_{\rm UV} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty
  \frac{k\,dk}{(k^2 + \gamma^2)^2}
  = \frac{1}{4\pi\gamma^2}\,.
\label{eq:IUV}
\end{equation}

\textit{IR integral} (Gribov propagator,
$D_{\rm GZ} = k^2/(k^4 + \gamma^4)$):
\begin{equation}
I_{\rm IR} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty
  \frac{k^5\,dk}{(k^4 + \gamma^4)^2}\,.
\label{eq:IIR}
\end{equation}
Substituting $u = k^2$, then $u = \gamma^2\tan\theta$:
\begin{align}
I_{\rm IR}
  &= \frac{1}{4\pi}\int_0^\infty
    \frac{u^2\,du}{(u^2 + \gamma^4)^2}
  \notag \\
  &= \frac{1}{4\pi\gamma^2}\int_0^{\pi/2}
    \sin^2\!\theta\;d\theta
  = \frac{1}{4\pi\gamma^2}\cdot\frac{\pi}{4}
  = \frac{1}{16\gamma^2}\,.
\label{eq:IIR_result}
\end{align}
The ratio is
\begin{equation}
\frac{I_{\rm IR}}{I_{\rm UV}}
  = \frac{1/(16\gamma^2)}{1/(4\pi\gamma^2)}
  = \frac{\pi}{4}\,,
\label{eq:ratio}
\end{equation}
as can be verified by direct evaluation. The Gribov mass $\gamma$
cancels in the ratio, yielding a universal screening factor.
